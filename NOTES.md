# Notes

Entry line (judged winner, candidate #7 of 8 in ../candidates.md — best score
across 3 blind judging rounds, ranked #1 in two of them):

> In the stairwell my dropped keys land three times: on the concrete, in the echo, and in the sudden quiet of the flat below.

Rationale: the judge's brief rewards, in order, a small everyday wonder caught
in a single sentence, an angle the reader has not met before where the phrasing
itself is the surprise, a surprise that lands in one read, grounding in
something real and specific, and no wasted words. This line takes the most
ordinary fumble there is — dropping your keys on the stairs — and stretches
that one clatter into three landings: the physical hit on the concrete, the
echo coming back off the stairwell walls, and then the turn that carries the
wonder, the sudden quiet of the flat below, where a stranger has stopped
whatever they were doing to listen. The sound quietly travels out of your own
moment and into someone else's, and the sentence ends inside their pause
rather than yours. Every element is real and specific (stairwell, keys,
concrete, echo, the flat below), the structure is a clean three-beat list that
resolves on the first read, and no word is decoration. 123 characters, one
sentence, no filler.

The brief's own examples (lift door opening on a song, the pause before a
kettle clicks, a puddle holding the sky) were deliberately avoided so the
entry does not echo the sponsor's illustrations.
